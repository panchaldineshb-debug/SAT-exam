### Question 1
**Difficulty:** Easy

A line in the xy-plane passes through the points (2, 5) and (6, 13). What is the slope of the line?

A) 1/2

B) 2

C) 4

D) 8

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the slope formula: `Slope = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)`.
2. Assign the coordinates from the given points: `(x1, y1) = (2, 5)` and `(x2, y2) = (6, 13)`.
3. Substitute these values into the slope formula: `(13 - 5) / (6 - 2)`.
4. Simplify the numerator and the denominator, and then divide to find the final slope.

### Question 2
**Difficulty:** Easy

A line has a slope of -3 and passes through the point (1, 4). Which of the following is the equation of the line?

A) y = -3x + 1

B) y = -3x + 4

C) y = -3x + 7

D) y = 3x - 7

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the slope-intercept form of a linear equation: `y = mx + b`, where `m` is the slope and `b` is the y-intercept.
2. Substitute the given slope `m = -3` into the equation: `y = -3x + b`.
3. To find the value of `b`, substitute the coordinates of the given point `(1, 4)` into the equation for `x` and `y`: `4 = -3(1) + b`.
4. Solve the equation for `b`.
5. Substitute the value of `b` back into `y = -3x + b` to get the final equation.

### Question 3
**Difficulty:** Easy

In the xy-plane, the line segment AB has one endpoint A at (3, -2) and its midpoint M at (5, 4). What are the coordinates of the other endpoint B?

A) (4, 1)

B) (7, 10)

C) (8, 6)

D) (13, 8)

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the midpoint formula: `M = ((x1 + x2) / 2, (y1 + y2) / 2)`.
2. Let the coordinates of point B be `(x2, y2)`. We know `(x1, y1) = (3, -2)` and the midpoint is `(5, 4)`.
3. Set up two separate equations for the x and y coordinates:
   - For x: `(3 + x2) / 2 = 5`
   - For y: `(-2 + y2) / 2 = 4`
4. Solve the first equation for `x2` by multiplying both sides by 2 and then subtracting 3.
5. Solve the second equation for `y2` by multiplying both sides by 2 and then adding 2.
6. The resulting `(x2, y2)` represents the coordinates of endpoint B.

### Question 4
**Difficulty:** Medium

Line k is defined by the equation 2x + 3y = 6. Line p is perpendicular to line k in the xy-plane and passes through the point (0, -4). Which of the following is the equation of line p?

A) y = -(2/3)x - 4

B) y = (2/3)x - 4

C) y = -(3/2)x - 4

D) y = (3/2)x - 4

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Write the equation of line k (`2x + 3y = 6`) in slope-intercept form (`y = mx + b`) to find its slope. Solve for `y` by subtracting `2x` from both sides and then dividing by 3.
2. Once you have the slope of line k (let's call it `m1`), recall that perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes: `m2 = -1 / m1`. Calculate the slope of line p.
3. The y-intercept of line p is given directly as the point `(0, -4)`, meaning `b = -4`.
4. Write the equation of line p using its slope and y-intercept in the form `y = mx + b`.

### Question 5
**Difficulty:** Medium

In the xy-plane, the line represented by the equation 4x - 5y = 20 intersects the x-axis at point (a, 0). What is the value of a?

A) -4

B) 0

C) 5

D) 20

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Understand that where a line intersects the x-axis (the x-intercept), the y-coordinate is always 0.
2. Substitute `y = 0` into the given equation: `4x - 5(0) = 20`.
3. Simplify the equation: `4x = 20`.
4. Solve for `x` to find the value of `a`.

### Question 6
**Difficulty:** Medium

A circle in the xy-plane is represented by the equation (x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 25. Which of the following correctly identifies the center and radius of the circle?

A) Center (-3, 4), radius 5

B) Center (3, -4), radius 5

C) Center (-3, 4), radius 25

D) Center (3, -4), radius 25

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the standard form equation of a circle: `(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2`, where `(h, k)` is the center of the circle and `r` is the radius.
2. Compare the given equation `(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 25` to the standard form:
   - Identify `h` from `(x - 3)^2`
   - Identify `k` from `(y + 4)^2` which can be rewritten as `(y - (-4))^2`
   - Identify `r^2` from `25`
3. Determine the center `(h, k)` and take the square root of `r^2` to find the radius `r`.

### Question 7
**Difficulty:** Medium

A circle in the xy-plane has the equation x^2 + y^2 - 6x + 8y - 11 = 0. What is the length of the radius of this circle?

A) 6

B) 11

C) 18

D) 36

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. To find the radius, convert the general form of the circle's equation into standard form `(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2` by completing the square for both x and y.
2. Group the x terms and the y terms, and move the constant term to the other side: `(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 8y) = 11`.
3. Complete the square for the x terms: add `(-6 / 2)^2 = 9` to both sides.
4. Complete the square for the y terms: add `(8 / 2)^2 = 16` to both sides.
5. Write the equation with the added terms: `(x^2 - 6x + 9) + (y^2 + 8y + 16) = 11 + 9 + 16`.
6. Factor the perfect square trinomials on the left and simplify the right side: `(x - 3)^2 + (y + 4)^2 = 36`.
7. Identify `r^2` from the right side of the equation and take its square root to find the radius `r`.

### Question 8
**Difficulty:** Medium

In the xy-plane, the lines y = 2x - 3 and y = -x + 6 intersect at the point P(a, b). What is the value of a + b?

A) 3

B) 5

C) 6

D) 9

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Find the point of intersection by setting the two equations equal to each other: `2x - 3 = -x + 6`.
2. Solve this linear equation for `x`. Add `x` to both sides and add 3 to both sides: `3x = 9`. Find the value of `x` (this is `a`).
3. Substitute the value of `x` back into either of the original line equations to find the corresponding `y` value (this is `b`).
4. Once you have the values for `a` and `b`, calculate their sum: `a + b`.

### Question 9
**Difficulty:** Hard

Which of the following points lies in the solution set of the system of inequalities shown below?
y > 2x - 1
y < -x + 4

A) (2, 4)

B) (3, -1)

C) (0, 0)

D) (1, 5)

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. To determine which point lies in the solution set, substitute the coordinates of each point `(x, y)` into both inequalities.
2. The correct point must make both inequalities true.
3. Test the coordinates for each option:
   - For A (2, 4): Is `4 > 2(2) - 1` AND is `4 < -2 + 4`?
   - For B (3, -1): Is `-1 > 2(3) - 1` AND is `-1 < -3 + 4`?
   - For C (0, 0): Is `0 > 2(0) - 1` AND is `0 < -0 + 4`?
   - For D (1, 5): Is `5 > 2(1) - 1` AND is `5 < -1 + 4`?
4. Find the option where both conditions are true.

### Question 10
**Difficulty:** Hard

A line segment AB has endpoints A(-2, 3) and B(4, 7). The segment is reflected across the y-axis, and then translated 3 units down to form segment A'B'. What are the coordinates of the midpoint of A'B'?

A) (-1, 2)

B) (1, 2)

C) (-1, 5)

D) (1, 8)

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. First, find the midpoint of the original segment AB using the midpoint formula: `((x1 + x2) / 2, (y1 + y2) / 2)`.
2. Let's call this original midpoint `M`. Calculate its coordinates: `((-2 + 4) / 2, (3 + 7) / 2)`.
3. Now apply the transformations directly to the midpoint `M` (since the midpoint of a transformed segment is the transformation of the original midpoint):
   - **Reflection across the y-axis:** Reflecting a point `(x, y)` across the y-axis changes the sign of the x-coordinate, resulting in `(-x, y)`. Apply this to `M`.
   - **Translation 3 units down:** Translating a point down by 3 units subtracts 3 from its y-coordinate, resulting in `(x, y - 3)`. Apply this to the reflected midpoint.
4. Alternatively, you can apply both transformations to points A and B to find the new endpoints A' and B', and then find the midpoint of A'B'. Both methods will give the same coordinates.
