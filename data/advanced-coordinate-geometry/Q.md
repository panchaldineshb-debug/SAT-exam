### Question 1
**Difficulty:** Easy

What is the distance between the points (1, 2) and (4, 6) in the xy-plane?

A) 3

B) 4

C) 5

D) 7

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the distance formula in the coordinate plane: `Distance = \sqrt{(x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2}`.
2. Substitute the coordinates of the two points `(1, 2)` and `(4, 6)` into the formula: `\sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (6 - 2)^2}`.
3. Simplify the terms inside the parentheses: `4 - 1 = 3` and `6 - 2 = 4`.
4. Square these values: `3^2 = 9` and `4^2 = 16`.
5. Add the squared values together: `9 + 16 = 25`.
6. Take the square root of 25 to find the distance.

### Question 2
**Difficulty:** Easy

A line passes through the point (0, 2) and is parallel to the line y = 4x - 7. Which of the following is the equation of the line?

A) y = -4x + 2

B) y = 4x - 2

C) y = 4x + 2

D) y = -(1/4)x + 2

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall that parallel lines have the exact same slope.
2. Identify the slope of the given line `y = 4x - 7` (which is 4).
3. The new line passes through `(0, 2)`. Since the x-coordinate is 0, this point is the y-intercept of the new line, meaning `b = 2`.
4. Write the equation of the new line in slope-intercept form `y = mx + b` using the slope `m = 4` and y-intercept `b = 2`.

### Question 3
**Difficulty:** Easy

A circle in the xy-plane is represented by the equation (x - 5)^2 + (y + 12)^2 = 49. What are the coordinates of the center of the circle?

A) (-5, 12)

B) (5, -12)

C) (-5, -12)

D) (5, 12)

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the standard equation of a circle: `(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2`, where `(h, k)` is the center of the circle.
2. Compare the given equation `(x - 5)^2 + (y + 12)^2 = 49` to the standard form:
   - Identify `h` from `(x - 5)^2`
   - Identify `k` from `(y + 12)^2` (which can be rewritten as `(y - (-12))^2`)
3. Write the center coordinates as `(h, k)`.

### Question 4
**Difficulty:** Medium

In the xy-plane, the midpoint of line segment AB is (3, 5). If the coordinates of point A are (-1, 2), what is the sum of the coordinates of point B?

A) 7

B) 8

C) 12

D) 15

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the midpoint formula: `Midpoint = ((x1 + x2) / 2, (y1 + y2) / 2)`.
2. Let the coordinates of point B be `(x, y)`. You are given `A(-1, 2)` and the midpoint `(3, 5)`.
3. Set up two separate equations to find the coordinates of B:
   - For x: `(-1 + x) / 2 = 3`
   - For y: `(2 + y) / 2 = 5`
4. Solve for `x` by multiplying by 2 and adding 1: `x = 7`.
5. Solve for `y` by multiplying by 2 and subtracting 2: `y = 8`.
6. Once you have the coordinates of B `(7, 8)`, calculate their sum: `7 + 8`.

### Question 5
**Difficulty:** Medium

Which of the following is the equation of the line that is perpendicular to y = -(1/2)x + 4 and passes through the point (2, 5)?

A) y = -2x + 9

B) y = 2x + 1

C) y = 2x + 5

D) y = -(1/2)x + 6

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Identify the slope of the given line: `m1 = -1/2`.
2. Recall that perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes: `m2 = -1 / m1`. Calculate the perpendicular slope: `-1 / (-1/2) = 2`.
3. Use the slope-intercept form `y = mx + b` with your new slope `m = 2`: `y = 2x + b`.
4. Substitute the coordinates of the point `(2, 5)` into the equation to find `b`: `5 = 2(2) + b` -> `5 = 4 + b`. Solve for `b`.
5. Write the final equation using `m = 2` and the value of `b` you calculated.

### Question 6
**Difficulty:** Medium

In the xy-plane, the line y = 3x - 2 intersects the parabola y = x^2 at two points. What is the sum of the x-coordinates of these two points of intersection?

A) -3

B) 2

C) 3

D) 5

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. To find the points of intersection, set the two equations equal to each other: `x^2 = 3x - 2`.
2. Move all terms to one side to form a standard quadratic equation: `x^2 - 3x + 2 = 0`.
3. Factor the quadratic equation: find two numbers that multiply to 2 and add to -3: `(x - r1)(x - r2) = 0`.
4. Solve for the two x-coordinates.
5. Add the two x-coordinate solutions together to find their sum.
6. (Alternative shortcut) Recall that for a quadratic equation `ax^2 + bx + c = 0`, the sum of the roots is given by `-b / a`. Identify `b = -3` and `a = 1` from Step 2 to find the sum directly.

### Question 7
**Difficulty:** Medium

A circle in the xy-plane has the equation x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 10y + k = 0, where k is a constant. If the radius of the circle is 6, what is the value of k?

A) 5

B) 25

C) 41

D) 77

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Convert the circle's equation into standard form `(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2` by completing the square.
2. Group the terms: `(x^2 - 8x) + (y^2 + 10y) = -k`.
3. Complete the square for the x terms: add `(-8 / 2)^2 = 16` to both sides.
4. Complete the square for the y terms: add `(10 / 2)^2 = 25` to both sides.
5. Write the equation with the added terms: `(x^2 - 8x + 16) + (y^2 + 10y + 25) = -k + 16 + 25` -> `(x - 4)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 41 - k`.
6. Since the radius is 6, the radius squared must be `6^2 = 36`.
7. Set the right side of your completed equation equal to 36: `41 - k = 36`. Solve for `k`.

### Question 8
**Difficulty:** Hard

What is the area of a triangle with vertices at (0, 0), (8, 0), and (4, 6) in the xy-plane?

A) 12

B) 24

C) 48

D) 96

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Plot the points conceptually to visualize the triangle:
   - Point 1 is at the origin `(0, 0)`.
   - Point 2 is at `(8, 0)` along the x-axis.
   - Point 3 is at `(4, 6)`.
2. Notice that the base of the triangle lies along the x-axis from `x = 0` to `x = 8`. Calculate the length of this base: `8 - 0 = 8` units.
3. The height of the triangle is the perpendicular distance from the third vertex `(4, 6)` to the x-axis. This corresponds to the y-coordinate of the third point. Identify this height (which is 6).
4. Use the triangle area formula: `Area = 0.5 * base * height`.
5. Calculate: `0.5 * 8 * 6`.

### Question 9
**Difficulty:** Hard

A parabola has its vertex at (3, -2) and passes through the point (5, 6). What is the y-value of the parabola when x = 1?

A) 2

B) 4

C) 6

D) 8

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Write the quadratic equation in vertex form: `y = a(x - h)^2 + k`.
2. Substitute the vertex `(3, -2)` into the equation: `y = a(x - 3)^2 - 2`.
3. Use the given point `(5, 6)` to solve for the coefficient `a`. Substitute `x = 5` and `y = 6` into the equation: `6 = a(5 - 3)^2 - 2`.
4. Simplify and solve for `a`: `6 = a(2)^2 - 2` -> `6 = 4a - 2` -> `8 = 4a`. Find `a`.
5. Rewrite the completed function: `y = a(x - 3)^2 - 2` with your calculated value of `a`.
6. Substitute `x = 1` into this completed equation and calculate the resulting `y` value.

### Question 10
**Difficulty:** Hard

The lines y = mx + 1 and y = 2x + 5 intersect at a point where the x-coordinate is positive. Which of the following could be the value of m?

A) -1

B) 0

C) 2

D) 3

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Find the x-coordinate of the intersection point by setting the two equations equal to each other: `mx + 1 = 2x + 5`.
2. Move the x terms to one side and constants to the other: `mx - 2x = 5 - 1`.
3. Factor out `x` on the left side: `x(m - 2) = 4`.
4. Solve for `x` in terms of `m`: `x = 4 / (m - 2)`.
5. The question states that the x-coordinate of the intersection is positive, meaning `x > 0`.
6. For the fraction `4 / (m - 2)` to be positive, the denominator must be positive: `m - 2 > 0`.
7. Solve the inequality to find the range of possible values for `m`.
8. Review the multiple-choice options to identify which value satisfies this inequality.
