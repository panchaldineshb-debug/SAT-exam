### Question 1
**Difficulty:** Easy

What is the radian measure of an angle that measures 120 degrees?

A) \pi/3

B) 2\pi/3

C) 3\pi/4

D) 5\pi/6

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the conversion factor from degrees to radians: multiply the degree measure by `\pi / 180`.
2. Set up the equation: `120 * (\pi / 180)`.
3. Simplify the fraction `120 / 180` by dividing the numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor (which is 60).
4. Multiply by `\pi` to get the final radian measure.

### Question 2
**Difficulty:** Easy

In a right-angled triangle ABC, the measure of angle C is 90 degrees. If cos(A) = 4/5, what is the value of sin(B)?

A) 3/5

B) 4/5

C) 1

D) 5/4

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the complementary angle relationship in trigonometry: for any acute angle `x` in a right triangle, `sin(90 - x) = cos(x)` and `cos(90 - x) = sin(x)`.
2. Since angle C is 90 degrees, the other two angles A and B are complementary: `A + B = 90`, which means `B = 90 - A`.
3. Substitute this into the sine function: `sin(B) = sin(90 - A)`.
4. Apply the complementary identity: `sin(90 - A) = cos(A)`.
5. Match the value of `cos(A)` given in the problem to find the value of `sin(B)`.

### Question 3
**Difficulty:** Easy

What is the sum of the measures of the interior angles of a regular hexagon?

A) 360 degrees

B) 540 degrees

C) 720 degrees

D) 1080 degrees

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the formula for the sum of the interior angles of any n-sided polygon: `Sum = (n - 2) * 180`, where `n` is the number of sides.
2. Identify the number of sides `n` in a hexagon (which is 6).
3. Substitute `n = 6` into the formula: `(6 - 2) * 180`.
4. Calculate the multiplication to find the sum.

### Question 4
**Difficulty:** Medium

A circle in the xy-plane is represented by the equation (x - 1)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16. If the circle is translated 4 units to the left and 2 units up, what is the equation of the translated circle?

A) (x - 5)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 16

B) (x + 3)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 16

C) (x + 3)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 16

D) (x - 5)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 16

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Find the center of the original circle: comparing `(x - 1)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16` to the standard form `(x - h)^2 + (y - k)^2 = r^2` gives the center `(h, k) = (1, -3)`.
2. Apply the translations to the center coordinates:
   - **4 units to the left:** subtract 4 from the x-coordinate: `1 - 4 = -3`.
   - **2 units up:** add 2 to the y-coordinate: `-3 + 2 = -1`.
   - The new center is `(h', k') = (-3, -1)`.
3. Note that translating a circle does not change its radius or radius squared, so `r^2` remains 16.
4. Write the new equation in standard form using the new center `(-3, -1)` and `r^2 = 16`: `(x - (-3))^2 + (y - (-1))^2 = 16`.
5. Simplify the double negatives.

### Question 5
**Difficulty:** Medium

An angle measures 3\pi/5 radians. What is the measure of this angle in degrees?

A) 36 degrees

B) 72 degrees

C) 108 degrees

D) 216 degrees

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the conversion factor from radians to degrees: multiply the radian measure by `180 / \pi`.
2. Set up the conversion: `(3\pi / 5) * (180 / \pi)`.
3. Cancel out the `\pi` terms from the numerator and denominator: `(3 / 5) * 180`.
4. Divide 180 by 5: `180 / 5 = 36`.
5. Multiply the result by 3: `3 * 36`.

### Question 6
**Difficulty:** Medium

In a regular octagon, what is the measure, in degrees, of a single interior angle?

A) 45 degrees

B) 135 degrees

C) 1080 degrees

D) 1440 degrees

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the formula for the sum of the interior angles of an n-sided polygon: `Sum = (n - 2) * 180`.
2. Identify the number of sides `n` in an octagon (which is 8).
3. Substitute `n = 8` into the formula: `(8 - 2) * 180 = 6 * 180 = 1080` degrees.
4. Since the octagon is regular, all 8 interior angles are equal.
5. Divide the sum of the interior angles (1080 degrees) by the number of angles (8) to find the measure of a single interior angle.

### Question 7
**Difficulty:** Medium

A right triangle has legs of length 8 and 15. What is the value of the cosine of the angle opposite the side of length 8?

A) 8/17

B) 15/17

C) 8/15

D) 15/8

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Find the length of the hypotenuse using the Pythagorean theorem: `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`.
2. Substitute the leg lengths: `8^2 + 15^2 = c^2` -> `64 + 225 = c^2` -> `289 = c^2`. Find the hypotenuse `c`.
3. Identify the angle: let `\theta` be the angle opposite the side of length 8.
4. Recall the trigonometric definition of cosine: `cos(\theta) = Adjacent / Hypotenuse`.
5. For the angle opposite the side of length 8, the adjacent side is the other leg (which has length 15), and the hypotenuse is the value calculated in Step 2.
6. Set up the fraction.

### Question 8
**Difficulty:** Hard

In circle O, the length of arc AB is 4\pi, and the area of sector AOB is 16\pi. What is the radius of the circle?

A) 4

B) 8

C) 12

D) 16

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Let the central angle of sector AOB be `\theta` degrees and the radius of the circle be `r`.
2. Set up the formula for arc length: `Arc Length = (\theta / 360) * 2\pi * r = 4\pi`.
3. Simplify this equation by dividing both sides by `2\pi`: `(\theta / 360) * r = 2`.
4. Set up the formula for sector area: `Sector Area = (\theta / 360) * \pi * r^2 = 16\pi`.
5. Simplify this equation by dividing both sides by `\pi`: `(\theta / 360) * r^2 = 16`.
6. Notice that the sector area equation can be written as: `[(\theta / 360) * r] * r = 16`.
7. Substitute the expression from Step 3 into the sector area equation: `[2] * r = 16`.
8. Solve for `r`.

### Question 9
**Difficulty:** Hard

If \theta is an acute angle and sin(\theta) = k, which of the following expressions represents cos(\theta) in terms of k?

A) 1 - k

B) 1 - k^2

C) \sqrt{1 - k^2}

D) k / \sqrt{1 - k^2}

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the fundamental Pythagorean trigonometric identity: `sin^2(\theta) + cos^2(\theta) = 1`.
2. Rearrange the identity to isolate `cos^2(\theta)`: `cos^2(\theta) = 1 - sin^2(\theta)`.
3. Substitute the given value `sin(\theta) = k` into the rearranged equation: `cos^2(\theta) = 1 - k^2`.
4. Take the square root of both sides to find `cos(\theta)`. Since `\theta` is an acute angle, its cosine must be positive, so we use the positive square root.

### Question 10
**Difficulty:** Hard

A right circular cylinder with radius r and height h has a volume of V. If the radius of the cylinder is doubled and the height is halved, what is the volume of the new cylinder in terms of V?

A) V/2

B) V

C) 2V

D) 4V

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Recall the volume formula of a cylinder: `V = \pi * r^2 * h`.
2. Write down the expressions for the new dimensions:
   - New radius = `2r`
   - New height = `h / 2`
3. Substitute these new dimensions into the cylinder volume formula: `V_new = \pi * (2r)^2 * (h / 2)`.
4. Simplify the squared term: `(2r)^2 = 4 * r^2`.
5. Rewrite the expression: `V_new = \pi * (4 * r^2) * (h / 2)`.
6. Rearrange and simplify the constants: `V_new = (4 / 2) * \pi * r^2 * h = 2 * (\pi * r^2 * h)`.
7. Replace the term `\pi * r^2 * h` with the original volume `V` to express `V_new` in terms of `V`.
