### Question 11 (Advanced — Cross-Text Connections & Scope Restrictions)

**Text 2**  
While Thorne’s findings highlight the utility of waking rest, they rely primarily on task performance measured immediately after rest intervals. Long-term retention of procedural tasks requires synaptic consolidation processes that predominantly occur during slow-wave sleep phases. Waking rest may facilitate temporary buffer storage, but it cannot fully substitute for the structural plastic changes induced by full sleep cycles.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the claim in Text 1 regarding memory stabilization?

A) By asserting that Thorne’s experimental setup fabricated neural reactivation data during daytime rest periods.  
B) By arguing that daytime rest only provides short-term stabilization rather than full long-term structural retention.  
C) By claiming that daytime rest intervals impair long-term memory formation compared to continuous physical activity.  
D) By suggesting that slow-wave sleep phases can be intentionally induced during 15-minute waking intervals.
