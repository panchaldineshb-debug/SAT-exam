# SAT Math Study Notes: Unit Circle Trigonometry & Coterminal Angles

This topic links directly to your school Algebra 2 curriculum (Trigonometric Functions & The Unit Circle).

---

## 1. The Unit Circle Basics
The unit circle is a circle with a radius of 1 centered at the origin (0, 0) of the coordinate plane.

### Coordinates and Trig Functions
For any angle (theta) starting on the positive x-axis and rotating counterclockwise:
*   **x-coordinate** of the intersection point is equal to **cos(theta)**.
*   **y-coordinate** of the intersection point is equal to **sin(theta)**.
*   **tangent** of the angle is equal to **y / x**, which is **sin(theta) / cos(theta)**.

---

## 2. Coterminal Angles (Angles larger than 360 degrees or 2*pi radians)
Angles that share the same terminal side are called coterminal. 

### Core Strategy: Subtracting Full Circles
Since a full circle is 360 degrees or 2*pi radians:
1.  **For degrees:** Subtract multiples of 360 until you get an angle between 0 and 360.
2.  **For radians:** Subtract multiples of 2*pi until you get an angle between 0 and 2*pi.

### Walkthrough Example (from Test 5, Q19):
*   **The Problem:** Find the tangent of the angle `92 * pi / 3`.
*   **Step 1:** Convert `92 / 3` into a mixed number:
    92 / 3 = 30 + 2/3
    So the angle is `30 * pi + (2 * pi / 3)`.
*   **Step 2:** Subtract multiples of 2*pi (full revolutions).
    Since 30*pi is 15 full revolutions (15 * 2*pi = 30*pi), we can subtract 30*pi completely.
    This leaves us with the simpler angle:
    `2 * pi / 3`
*   **Step 3:** Locate the coterminal angle on the unit circle.
    `2 * pi / 3` (or 120 degrees) is in the second quadrant.
    The coordinates for `2 * pi / 3` on the unit circle are:
    x = -1/2
    y = sqrt(3) / 2
*   **Step 4:** Calculate the tangent:
    tan = y / x
    tan = [sqrt(3) / 2] / [-1/2]
    Multiply by the reciprocal:
    tan = [sqrt(3) / 2] * [-2 / 1]
    tan = -sqrt(3)

    *   **Quadrant II (Students):** only sin is positive (cos, tan are negative).
    *   **Quadrant III (Take):** only tan is positive (sin, cos are negative).
    *   **Quadrant IV (Calculus):** only cos is positive (sin, tan are negative).
