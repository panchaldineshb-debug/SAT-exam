# SAT Verbal Study Notes: Text Structure, Purpose & Transitions

## 1. Text Structure & Purpose
These questions ask you to identify the main role of a specific sentence (often underlined) or how the entire passage is organized.

### Core Strategies
*   **The "Why" vs. "What" Rule:** Do not just look at *what* the sentence says. Ask *why* the author put it there. 
    *   Does it introduce a hypothesis?
    *   Does it present a counterexample?
    *   Does it offer a concession?
*   **Structural Anchors:** Look for transition words right before or within the underlined sentence. Words like *however*, *consequently*, or *for example* dictate the sentence's function in the passage.
*   **Summarize in Your Own Words First:** Read the passage, cover the choices, and state what the paragraph is doing in one simple sentence (e.g., "The author starts with a theory, then shows a study that disproves it").

### Common SAT Traps
*   **The Half-Right Trap:** A choice describes part of the sentence correctly but misrepresents its overall purpose in the passage.
*   **Factual but Irrelevant:** The choice contains information that is true according to the passage, but it does NOT describe the *function* of the underlined sentence.

## 2. Transition Questions
These questions ask you to choose the most logical word or phrase to connect two sentences.

### Core Strategies
*   **Categorize the Transition:** Every transition word falls into one of three main categories:
    1.  **Cause/Effect (Result):** *As a result*, *therefore*, *consequently*, *hence*, *accordingly*.
    2.  **Contrast (Opposite):** *However*, *on the contrary*, *conversely*, *nevertheless*, *though*.
    3.  **Addition/Illustration:** *In addition*, *for example*, *furthermore*, *specifically*, *for instance*.
*   **Read WITHOUT the Transition First:** Read the sentence before and the sentence after without looking at the blank. Determine the logical relationship (Are they matching? Opposing? Is the second a result of the first?).
*   **Eliminate Synonyms:** If two choices mean the exact same thing (e.g., *therefore* and *consequently*), both are incorrect. You can immediately eliminate them!
