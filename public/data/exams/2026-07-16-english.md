### Question 1
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Domain:** Craft and Structure (Cross-Text Connections)

**Text 1**
Many paleontologists believe that standard models of dinosaur social behavior are fundamentally flawed because they rely too heavily on isolated fossil finds. When bones of multiple individuals are found in close proximity, researchers often jump to the conclusion that they represents a family unit or a cohesive pack. However, these collections could easily be the result of random gatherings, such as solitary animals drawn to a shrinking water source during a drought.

**Text 2**
A team of archaeologists recently excavated a mass grave containing the fossilized remains of over twenty *Sinornithomimus* dinosaurs of varying ages. Analysis of the surrounding sediment indicates that all the dinosaurs died simultaneously in a single mud-trap event. Because the group consists of juveniles and adults of the same species who perished together, the researchers argue this provides strong evidence that *Sinornithomimus* lived in structured, multi-generational herds.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 1 most likely respond to the researchers' argument in Text 2?

A) By pointing out that a single mass grave does not disprove the possibility that most other dinosaur species were solitary.

B) By welcoming the discovery as a more reliable basis for inferring social behavior than isolated fossil finds.

C) By claiming that the individuals in the mass grave were likely solitary scavengers rather than a cohesive herd.

D) By suggesting that the sediment analysis conducted by the researchers was flawed.

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Identify the core objection in Text 1: The author complains that paleontologists infer social behavior from "isolated fossil finds" where bones are merely "in close proximity," ignoring the possibility that they gathered randomly over time (e.g., at a water source).
2. Examine the evidence in Text 2: The researchers found a mass grave where over twenty individuals died *simultaneously* in a single event.
3. Compare the two: Does the "simultaneous death in a single event" address the objection in Text 1 about random gatherings over time? Yes, it shows they were physically together at the exact moment of death, which is a stronger, non-isolated form of evidence.
4. Select the option that reflects how the author of Text 1 would view this stronger evidence compared to the "isolated fossil finds" they criticized. Avoid options making extreme, unsupported claims (like C or D).

### Question 2
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Domain:** Craft and Structure (Cross-Text Connections)

**Text 1**
For decades, the dominant archaeological view asserted that the transition to agriculture in ancient Mesoamerica was a rapid revolution. Proponents of this "rapid transition" model argued that sudden, severe climate changes forced foraging communities to domesticate crops within a few generations to avoid starvation, leading to a swift abandonment of wild resource gathering.

**Text 2**
Recent botanical excavations in the Tehuacán Valley challenge old assumptions. The data show that Mesoamerican populations cultivated maize and squash as minor dietary supplements while continuing to rely on hunting and gathering for over 70% of their food for thousands of years. Agriculture was not a sudden emergency response but rather a slow, gradual integration into an existing mobile foraging lifestyle.

Based on the texts, how does the view of the transition to agriculture in Text 2 differ from the traditional view described in Text 1?

A) Text 2 describes the transition as a slow, gradual process rather than a rapid, emergency abandonment of foraging.

B) Text 2 suggests that Mesoamerican populations rejected agricultural techniques entirely.

C) Text 2 argues that the transition occurred even faster than the traditional model suggests.

D) Text 2 claims that agriculture arose in response to a sudden surplus of wild resources rather than climate-driven starvation.

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Summarize the traditional view from Text 1: The transition was a "rapid revolution" occurring in "a few generations" due to "sudden climate changes."
2. Summarize the new view from Text 2: The transition was "slow" and "gradual," where crops were "minor dietary supplements" while foraging continued for "thousands of years."
3. Compare the two summaries to find the key difference.
4. Choose the option that accurately describes this contrast without exaggerating either text's claims.

### Question 3
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Domain:** Craft and Structure (Cross-Text Connections)

**Text 1**
The introduction of non-native plant species into an ecosystem is universally detrimental. These invasive species inevitably outcompete native flora for sunlight, water, and soil nutrients. Over time, this leads to a drastic reduction in local biodiversity and disrupts the food webs that support native wildlife.

**Text 2**
In a study of degraded industrial sites, Ecologist Dr. Elena Rostova found that the non-native plant *Betula pendula* (silver birch) played a vital role in ecological recovery. The trees stabilized the eroded, metal-contaminated soil and accumulated toxic heavy metals in their roots, rendering the surrounding ground hospitable for native grasses and shrubs to return.

What is the relationship between the two texts?

A) Text 2 presents empirical evidence that directly supports the broad claim made in Text 1.

B) Text 2 introduces a specific scenario that qualifies the universal claim made in Text 1.

C) Text 2 refutes Text 1 by showing that non-native species are always beneficial to ecosystems.

D) Text 2 outlines a methodology for successfully eradicating non-native species.

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Look at the claim in Text 1: The introduction of non-native species is "universally detrimental."
2. Look at the finding in Text 2: In certain degraded industrial sites, a non-native plant (*Betula pendula*) actually helped ecological recovery and made the ground hospitable for native species.
3. Determine how the specific case in Text 2 interacts with the universal rule in Text 1: It serves as an exception or qualification (a case where a non-native species was *not* detrimental).
4. Evaluate the options to find the one that best describes Text 2 qualifying or showing an exception to the claim in Text 1.

### Question 4
**Difficulty:** Advanced (Hard)
**Domain:** Craft and Structure (Cross-Text Connections)

**Text 1**
Micro-finance initiatives—which provide small loans to low-income individuals, particularly women, in developing nations—have been widely celebrated by international development agencies. Advocates argue that these loans foster entrepreneurship, allow individuals to build small businesses, and ultimately pave a reliable path out of poverty while empowering women within their communities.

**Text 2**
While micro-finance initiatives certainly increase access to capital, studies show their long-term benefits are frequently overstated. Without broader structural reforms, such as investments in education and infrastructure, small loans rarely lead to sustainable business growth. Instead, they often burden recipients with high-interest debt and increase the domestic workload of women, who must manage both their households and new micro-enterprises under intense financial pressure.

How would the author of Text 2 most likely characterize the "advocates' argument" described in Text 1?

A) As well-intentioned but overly optimistic because it fails to account for potential negative outcomes and systemic limitations.

B) As entirely fabricated and driven by a desire to exploit low-income borrowers for profit.

C) As an accurate representation of micro-finance outcomes that holds true in the vast majority of cases.

D) As a harmful distraction that has successfully prevented governments from investing in infrastructure.

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Identify the advocates' argument in Text 1: Micro-finance is a "reliable path out of poverty" and "empowers women."
2. Identify the author of Text 2's perspective: The benefits are "frequently overstated." Without structural reforms, loans rarely lead to growth. They can cause "high-interest debt" and "increase the domestic workload of women."
3. Evaluate the tone of Text 2: The author does not call micro-finance a scam or say it is entirely useless (they acknowledge it "increases access to capital"). Rather, they point out its limitations and negative side effects.
4. Match this moderate, critical perspective with the correct option. Avoid extreme options (like B or D) that are not supported by the passage text.

### Question 5
**Difficulty:** Advanced (Hard)
**Domain:** Craft and Structure (Cross-Text Connections)

**Text 1**
The "hygiene hypothesis" suggests that the rapid rise in allergies and autoimmune disorders in modern urban environments is due to a lack of childhood exposure to microbes. In highly sanitized homes, children do not encounter the bacteria, viruses, and parasites that historically trained the human immune system to distinguish between actual pathogens and harmless substances like pollen or food proteins.

**Text 2**
While childhood exposure to diverse microbes does help regulate the immune system, attributing the rise of allergies solely to cleanliness is an oversimplification. Recent studies show that exposure to traffic-related air pollution and synthetic chemical toxins in modern cities disrupts the epithelial barriers of the lungs and skin. This barrier damage makes the immune system hyper-reactive, suggesting that pollution is just as significant a driver of the allergy epidemic as sanitization.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 respond to the "hygiene hypothesis" presented in Text 1?

A) By arguing that the hypothesis is invalid because sanitized environments actually contain higher levels of harmful bacteria.

B) By suggesting that the hypothesis is incomplete because it ignores other environmental factors like modern pollution.

C) By asserting that the rise of allergies is entirely genetic and unaffected by cleanliness or pollution.

D) By agreeing that sanitization is the sole cause of the rise in autoimmune disorders.

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Summarize the hygiene hypothesis from Text 1: Rise in allergies is due to a lack of childhood exposure to microbes (cleanliness).
2. Summarize Text 2's critique: Attributing the rise *solely* to cleanliness is an "oversimplification." Other modern factors like "air pollution and synthetic chemical toxins" are "just as significant a driver."
3. Identify the relationship: Text 2 doesn't say Text 1 is completely wrong (it agrees that microbial exposure helps), but argues it is too narrow (incomplete) because it leaves out pollution.
4. Select the option that describes this "incomplete/needs expansion" stance.

### Question 6
**Difficulty:** Advanced (Hard)
**Domain:** Craft and Structure (Cross-Text Connections)

**Text 1**
Translators of classical literature must prioritize literal fidelity above all else. A translation is only as good as its adherence to the exact syntax, vocabulary, and structural layout of the source text. Deviating from the original wording to make the text sound more modern or readable is a disservice to the author, as it replaces their authentic voice with the translator's own stylistic preferences.

**Text 2**
A translation that achieves literal word-for-word accuracy often loses the very essence of the original work. Idioms, cultural references, and rhythmic nuances do not translate directly across languages; a rigid adherence to source syntax frequently produces awkward, unreadable prose that fails to evoke the same emotional response in modern readers. To truly preserve the author's voice, translators must employ "dynamic equivalence"—translating the meaning, tone, and impact rather than just the words.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 respond to the priority asserted in Text 1?

A) By arguing that prioritizing literal fidelity can result in a text that fails to convey the emotional and cultural impact of the original.

B) By agreeing that literal fidelity is the best way to preserve the authentic voice of the author.

C) By claiming that a translator's own stylistic preferences are more important than the author's original meaning.

D) By suggesting that dynamic equivalence is only necessary when translating modern literature, not classical works.

**Hint / Guided Steps:**
1. Identify the priority in Text 1: Literal fidelity (exact syntax, vocabulary, layout) is the highest priority.
2. Identify the response of Text 2: Literal accuracy loses the "essence," producing "awkward, unreadable prose" that "fails to evoke the same emotional response."
3. Synthesize the reaction: The author of Text 2 would argue that focusing too much on literal translation hurts the reader's ability to experience the text's true meaning and emotional/cultural impact.
4. Match this with the correct answer option.
