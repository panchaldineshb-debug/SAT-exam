### 🚨 The SAT Reading Golden Rules:
1. **The Poison Word Scan:** The moment you see words like *never*, *always*, *entirely*, *completely*, *impossible*, *solely*, or *all*, treat them as glowing red danger signs. Cross them out unless the passage explicitly used that exact absolute claim.
2. **The Boring / Defensible Answer Rule:** The College Board loves moderate, cautious, defensible phrasing (*may*, *contributes to*, *does not fully account for*, *partially explains*, *is not universal*). When between two choices, the more nuanced and measured answer is almost always the winner.
3. **The Target / Relationship Check:** Make sure your answer accurately describes the *specific relationship* between texts or the *exact claim* in the passage—don't stop at a half-right statement.

### Question 7 (Cross-Text Connections / Subtle Modifiers)
**Text 1**
Behavioral psychologists have argued that intrinsic motivation—engaging in an activity for its own inherent joy—is inevitably undermined whenever extrinsic tangible rewards (such as cash bonuses or letter grades) are offered. This phenomenon, known as the "overjustification effect," suggests that paying students to read books diminishes their natural curiosity and destroys their long-term reading habits.

**Text 2**
Comprehensive meta-analyses of classroom incentive structures demonstrate that the overjustification effect is highly contingent on students' baseline interest. When students already possess high intrinsic interest in a subject, external rewards can indeed dampen self-directed motivation. However, for students who initially exhibit negligible interest or severe skill deficits, structured extrinsic rewards frequently provide the necessary initial scaffolding, building baseline competence that subsequently sparks authentic intrinsic interest.

Based on the texts, how would the author of Text 2 most likely respond to the claim in Text 1 that extrinsic rewards inevitably destroy intrinsic motivation?
A) By asserting that extrinsic rewards are entirely superior to intrinsic motivation in every educational setting.
B) By claiming that intrinsic motivation is a psychological myth that plays no meaningful role in student achievement.
C) By arguing that the overjustification effect never occurs under any experimental conditions.
D) By contending that the overjustification effect is not universal, as extrinsic incentives can help establish initial engagement among students who initially lack motivation.

### Question 8 (Scientific Inference / Complex Data Interpretation)

Which choice most logically completes the text?
A) proves that adult primate brains are entirely incapable of structural neuroplasticity.

However, subsequent long-term research in other temperate parks noted that where river channels had already deeply incised and local water tables had dropped permanently prior to predator reintroduction, simply returning apex predators failed to restore riparian vegetation without active hydrological engineering.

Throughout history, West African musicians eagerly integrated foreign instruments—from Portuguese stringed guitars in the 16th century to American brass horns and funk basslines in the 1970s—without sacrificing their core polyrhythmic complexity. Treating tradition as an immutable relic frozen in the pre-colonial era fundamentally misinterprets the living, adaptive nature of African cultural expression.

Based on the texts, how would the composers in Text 2 most likely respond to Dr. Osei's argument in Text 1?
A) By arguing that acoustic instruments and traditional folkloric rhythms should be completely abandoned in modern Africa.
B) By claiming that digital synthesizers have made all historical polyrhythmic traditions entirely obsolete.
C) By asserting that unamplified folkloric music was never appreciated by traditional West African audiences.
D) By maintaining that incorporating modern electronic elements is consistent with a centuries-old tradition of artistic adaptation and cultural dynamism.
