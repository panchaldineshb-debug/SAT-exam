# SAT Prep: Linear Equation System - Difficulty Levels Guide (difficulty_levels.md)

This guide defines the characteristics of the three difficulty levels (**Easy**, **Medium**, and **Hard**) for the **Linear Equation System** topic on the SAT exam, complete with examples from the practice set.

---

## 1. Easy Difficulty

Easy questions test direct recall of definitions and basic algebraic operations. They usually require only 1 or 2 steps to solve.

### Key Characteristics:
*   **Direct Elimination / Addition:** The equations are formatted so that adding or subtracting them immediately eliminates a variable without needing to multiply first.
*   **Simple Substitution:** One variable is already isolated, allowing for quick, direct substitution.
*   **Evaluating a Point:** You are given the solution coordinates `(x, y)` and must plug them directly into one of the equations to find a missing coefficient.
*   **Visual Slope Comparison:** Comparing parallel lines where both equations are already written in slope-intercept form (`y = mx + b`), allowing you to read the slope directly.

### Practice Exam Examples:
*   **Question 1:** Solve `x + 2y = 10` and `3x - 2y = 6`. (Easy because adding them eliminates `y` instantly).
*   **Question 2:** If `(3, 2)` is the solution to `ax - 2y = 8`, find `a`. (Easy because you directly substitute `x = 3` and `y = 2`).
*   **Question 3:** `y = -2x + 7` and `y = kx - 3` have no solution. Find `k`. (Easy because you compare the slopes directly: `k = -2`).
*   **Question 12:** Solve `x = y - 3` and `2x + 3y = 14`. (Easy because the first equation already has `x` isolated for substitution).

---

## 2. Medium Difficulty

Medium questions require a bit of planning and strategy. They often require scaling equations (multiplication) or translating simple word problems.

### Key Characteristics:
*   **Multi-Step Elimination:** You cannot add or subtract the equations immediately. You must first multiply one (or both) equations by a constant to align the coefficients.
*   **Simple Word Problems:** Translating straightforward real-world scenarios (like total item count and total cost) into a system of equations.
*   **Combination Shortcuts:** Finding a specific expression (like `x + y` or `5x - 2y`) where adding or subtracting the equations yields the expression after a simple division or factoring step.
*   **Simple Graph Intersections:** Finding the intersection point of two lines and then using that point in a third line.

### Practice Exam Examples:
*   **Question 4:** `2` coffees and `3` muffins cost `$14.50`, and `4` coffees and `1` muffin cost `$14.00`. Find the cost of `1` muffin. (Medium because you must multiply the first equation by `2` to eliminate the coffee variable).
*   **Question 5:** If `4x + y = 17` and `x + 4y = 13`, find `x + y`. (Medium because adding them gives `5x + 5y = 30`, which requires factoring out 5 to get `x + y = 6`).
*   **Question 6:** Find `c` if `y = 2x - 3` and `4x - 2y = c` have infinitely many solutions. (Medium because you must rewrite the first equation in standard form first).

---

## 3. Hard Difficulty

Hard questions test conceptual depth, geometric interpretations, and advanced problem-solving skills. They contain multiple steps, complex word translation, or abstract constants.

### Key Characteristics:
*   **Constants as Coefficients (Parametric Systems):** The equations include unknown constants (like `a`, `b`, or `k`) in place of numbers. You must use conditions like "no solution" or "infinitely many solutions" to solve for them.
*   **Coordinate Geometry Intersections:** Systems of equations intersecting with coordinate axes (e.g., crossing at the x-axis, meaning `y = 0`).
*   **Complex Word Problems:** Problems involving mixtures (percentages) or place-value digits (reversing two-digit numbers).
*   **Non-Standard Targets:** Solving the system for both variables and then computing a custom expression like `xy` or `a + b`.

### Practice Exam Examples:
*   **Question 7:** If `ax - 5y = 8` and `3x - 2y = 12` have no solution, find `a`. (Hard because you must equate the ratio of coefficients: `a/3 = -5/-2`).
*   **Question 8:** The lines `3x - y = 5` and `ax + 2y = 8` intersect on the x-axis. Find `a`. (Hard because you must recognize that `y = 0` at the intersection, solve for `x`, and then substitute `(x, 0)` into the second equation).
*   **Question 9:** Saline mixture problem (mixing `10%` and `25%` solutions to get `30` liters of `15%`). (Hard due to decimal translation and multiple algebraic steps).
*   **Question 20:** Two-digit number digit reversal problem. (Hard because it requires representing a number by its place values: `10t + u` and `10u + t`).
